Abstract —Bitemporal remote sensing change detection re-quires representations that preserve spatial detail while capturing contextual information relevant to changes between acquisitions. Visual test-time training offers an alternative to explicit token-to-token attention by adapting a compact inner model to each image. This letter describes ChangeT 3, a vanilla Siamese configuration combining the existing hierarchical ViT 3-Tiny encoder with the multiscale ChangeFormer decoder. The two image streams share learned encoder parameters, while their fast weights are com-puted independently from their respective spatial features. Each encoder block retains the inherited global simplified SwiGLU and local adaptive depthwise-convolution branches. Temporal comparison is performed by the decoder through learned fusion of paired features at four resolutions, followed by coarse-to-fine aggregation and binary prediction. This separation provides a direct setting for examining image-conditioned inner updates without introducing an additional temporal interaction module. Trained from scratch on LEVIR-CD+ with the same decoder, recipe, and budget as a ChangeFormer control, ChangeT 3 reaches an F1 of 80.61 ±0.90 against 78.84 ±0.21 for ChangeFormer (three seeds each); as in the standard protocol for this dataset, checkpoints are selected on the test split. Trained with both inner updates disabled, the same encoder reaches 78.63 ±0.20. Neither model transfers zero-shot to SYSU-CD, DSIFN-CD, or WHU-CD, and ChangeT 3 is the slower of the two at small inputs despite similar parameter and operation counts. 

Index Terms —Remote sensing, change detection, test-time training, Siamese networks, multiscale features. 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

# BUILDING change detection from bitemporal remote sensing imagery supports the monitoring of urban de-velopment and the updating of geographic information. Given two co-registered observations, the objective is to distinguish changes in the target structures from variations in appearance that do not alter their change labels. Illumination, shadows, seasonal conditions, and differences in acquisition geometry can complicate this distinction. Accurate dense prediction therefore requires both localized structural information and a representation of the surrounding scene. Building-change benchmarks such as LEVIR-CD and its extension provide a setting for studying this problem [1], [2]. Siamese encoders provide a natural starting point by ex-tracting features from each date using shared parameters. Fully convolutional Siamese networks established practical early- and feature-fusion formulations [3]. Subsequent work has sought richer contextual and object-level representations. For example, DTCDSCN combines change detection with semantic segmentation supervision [4]. Transformer-based ap-proaches offer another route to contextual reasoning. BIT summarizes bitemporal features into a compact set of semantic tokens [5], whereas ChangeFormer combines a hierarchical Transformer encoder with multiscale learned difference fea-tures and a decoder [6]. Semantic change detection, which also labels the land-cover classes involved, has added attention modules and a Dice loss to SCanNet [7] and combined spatio-frequency feature fusion with change-guided attention [8]. These designs illustrate that the representation used before temporal comparison is a central architectural choice. The cost of spatial context modeling also matters for high-resolution inputs. Full self-attention forms pairwise relations between tokens, although practical architectures can reduce this cost through token reduction or other restrictions. Linear-complexity alternatives have consequently attracted attention in change detection. LSAT uses cross-dimension interactions and structure-aware enhancement [9]; ChangeMamba inves-tigates state-space modeling for spatial and temporal rep-resentations [10], and precision fusion blocks added to it capture channel-wise temporal variations and per-pixel differ-ences [11]; MambaRefine-CD pairs a MambaVision encoder with region-boundary temporal refinement [12]; and ChangeR-WKV combines a hierarchical RWKV encoder with spatial-temporal fusion [13]. Their differing fusion mechanisms mean that an alternative token mixer must be assessed as part of a clearly specified change-detection pipeline, rather than being credited with improvements demonstrated by another architecture. Controlled benchmarks that hold everything but the backbone fixed, and that also report boundary quality and behavior under domain shift, make such an assessment possible [14]. Test-time training (TTT) was introduced to adapt a model to each test input through a self-supervised auxiliary task [15], [16], alongside fully test-time adaptation methods that update a deployed model without labels [17], [18]. The visual ViT 3 architecture adapts this principle to images through full-batch spatial updates and complementary global and local inner models [24]. However, per-image adaptation does not itself compare acquisitions: independently adapted date features must still be combined to produce a change map. Whether this representation is ben-eficial for building-change detection is therefore an empirical question. This letter develops a deliberately unmodified encoder– decoder configuration for examining that question. ChangeT 3

uses a shared hierarchical ViT 3-Tiny encoder and the existing ChangeFormer V6 decoder. Each date generates its own fast weights; no temporal exchange is inserted into the encoder. The method description distinguishes these image-conditioned weights from shared learned parameters and identifies the decoder as the point of temporal fusion. The accompanying evaluation structure isolates the global and local inner up-dates while retaining the decoder. These are an architectural adaptation and an experimental study design, rather than new inner models or a new temporal-fusion mechanism. On LEVIR-CD+, the encoder with active inner updates exceeds a ChangeFormer control that shares its decoder, recipe, and budget, whereas the same encoder trained without updates does not (Section III). That advantage does not carry over to zero-shot evaluation on other datasets, where every model trained only on LEVIR-CD+ fails (Section III-E). II. M ETHOD

Ai = Pi(F iA), Bi = Pi(F iB ). (8) All runs use D = 256 . A learned convolutional differ-ence block ϕi transforms the ordered concatenation [Ai, B i].Coarse-to-fine decoding is

D. Computational Scope 

For fixed channel dimensions and one inner update, the global inner-model operations scale as O(N hd 2) and the local kernel operations as O(N dk 2), with k = 3 . Channel projec-tions and the spatial feed-forward operations are also linear in 

N at fixed widths. Applying the shared encoder twice doubles its per-image work without doubling its learned parameters. This inherited token-linear structure avoids constructing a full 

N × N attention matrix, but it does not by itself establish lower measured latency, memory use, or parameter count than a competing detector. End-to-end profiling must include both date passes, inner-update arithmetic, and decoding. III. E XPERIMENTS 

A. Dataset and Evaluation Protocol 

The benchmark is LEVIR-CD+, the extended building-change dataset linked by the LEVIR authors [1], [2], in its 256 ×256 patch release: each 1024 ×1024 scene pair is cut into 16 non-overlapping patches with stride 256. The 637 training scenes yield 10,192 training patches and the other 348 scenes yield 5,568 test patches, so training and test scenes are disjoint. In this release, as in the literature that uses it, the validation set is a byte-identical copy of the test set (identical file lists and mask checksums). Every score reported here is therefore a validation score in the strict sense, and no independent test estimate exists. Images are normalized with ImageNet statistics, masks are binarized at 127, and no data augmentation is applied. Change-class precision, recall, F1, and intersection over union (IoU) will summarize pixel-level predictions. C. Controlled Inner-Update Ablations 

Table III defines a planned factorial study of the global and local inner updates. Setting a step size to zero retains that branch and its learned initialization; it does not remove the branch. Independently training each configuration tests adaptation within the learning system, whereas changing a step size only at inference probes reliance of a particular checkpoint on its updates. These protocols will be reported separately; Table III covers the first. All four configurations use seed 42 and are otherwise identi-cal. Training with both updates gives an F1 of 81.64, the global update alone 80.18, the local update alone 80.80, and neither 78.73. Relative to no update, the global update alone is worth 1.45 points, the local update alone 2.07, and both together 2.91. Seed 42 is the strongest of the three ChangeT 3 seeds, so the seed means are the fairer comparison: 80.61 ±0.90 with both updates against 78.63 ±0.20 without, a gap of 1.97 points that is well outside either spread. Either update alone already recovers most of that gap. The single-branch runs have one seed each and fall within the full model’s seed spread, so these data do not separate the two branches. Without updates, ChangeT 3 scores at the level of ChangeFormer (78.63 against 78.84).

E. Zero-Shot Cross-Domain Evaluation 

Following domain-shift analyses of remote-sensing back-bones [14], every model trained on LEVIR-CD+ is also evaluated, without any tuning, on the test splits of SYSU-CD (4,000 pairs), DSIFN-CD (48), and WHU-CD (762). The checkpoint is the one selected on LEVIR-CD+ and the decision is the argmax of the two-class logits; nothing is selected or calibrated on a target. SYSU-CD and WHU-CD pairs are 256 ×256 and used as they are. DSIFN-CD pairs are 512 ×512; they are cut into four non-overlapping 256 ×256 tiles at native resolution, and the stitched predictions are scored on the full images. Resizing them to 256 ×256 instead, as earlier code bases do, lowers every model’s score and leaves the conclusions unchanged. Table IV reports the scores. Every model trained from scratch is near the floor on every target: the mean F1 over the three targets is 6.64 for ChangeFormer, 3.57 for ChangeT 3

without updates, and 1.83 for ChangeT 3, so the inner update that helps in-domain does not help here, and ChangeT 3 is the lowest of the three. The failure is one of recall. On SYSU-CD, ChangeT 3 marks 0.20% of the pixels as changed against 23.6% in the reference, with a precision of 0.68 and a recall of 0.006; DSIFN-CD and WHU-CD show the same pattern. Differences in what each dataset labels as change may contribute on SYSU-CD and DSIFN-CD, whose labels cover more than buildings, but WHU-CD labels building changes as LEVIR-CD+ does and shows the same collapse, so label definitions alone do not explain it. Their latency is not similar: at batch size 1 and 256 ×256, ChangeT 3 takes 89.9 ms against 16.2 ms, and at batch size 8 it processes 50.4 pairs per second against 79.1. Skipping the inner-gradient computation, which leaves the output equal to η=0 , shows that the update adds 49% to the batch-1 latency and 23% to the batch-8 time, with no measurable extra memory. The gap narrows with input size (Table VI), from 5.1 × at 256 ×256 to 1.7 × at 1024 ×1024, while operations and memory stay close: at small inputs the cost is dominated by the number of operations launched rather than by their arithmetic. Timings on this shared machine also drifted within a profiling run, as identical architectures measured at different points differed by up to 2.4 × in batch-1 latency and 15% in batch-8 throughput. The latencies are therefore indicative, and the parameter, operation, and memory counts are the reliable comparison. IV. C ONCLUSION 

This letter specifies a vanilla adaptation of hierarchical visual TTT to bitemporal change detection using an exist-ing multiscale ChangeFormer decoder. Its defining separation is between shared learned encoder parameters, independent image-conditioned fast weights, and decoder-based temporal comparison. The architecture provides a setting for evaluating the contribution of the inherited global and local inner updates without adding temporal modules. On LEVIR-CD+, with the decoder, recipe, and budget held fixed, the ViT 3 encoder with active inner updates reaches an F1 of 80.61 ±0.90 against 78.84 ±0.21 for the ChangeFormer encoder over three seeds each, while the same encoder trained with both updates disabled reaches 78.63 ±0.20, level with ChangeFormer. The advantage stays in-domain: evaluated zero-shot on SYSU-CD, DSIFN-CD, and WHU-CD, every scratch model fails through missed changes, ChangeT 3 most of all, and pretraining helps where the inner update does not. At similar parameter and operation counts, ChangeT 3 is slower than ChangeFormer at small inputs, and the difference narrows at larger ones. These results rest on one training dataset, one baseline, and checkpoints selected on the test split; the controls are correla-tional evidence about the inner updates, not a causal account.
