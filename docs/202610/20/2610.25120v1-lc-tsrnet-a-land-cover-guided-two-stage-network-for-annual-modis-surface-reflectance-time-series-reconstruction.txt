LC-TSRNet: A Land-Cover-Guided Two-Stage
Network for Annual MODIS Surface Reflectance
Time Series Reconstruction
Wenjuan Zhang , Member, IEEE, Xuesong Ma , Zhen Li , Meixiang Wang , Caimeng Yi , Jie Pan ,
Nan Su , Member, IEEE, and Bing Zhang , Fellow, IEEE
Abstract—Moderate Resolution Imaging Spectroradiometer
(MODIS) surface reflectance (SR) products often suffer from data
gaps due to cloud contamination and sensor malfunctions, limiting
their applications in long-term and large-scale studies. Most
existing methods rely on cloud-free observations from optical time
series or introduce synthetic aperture radar (SAR) time series as
auxiliary information. However, due to the coarse spatial resolution
of MODIS, cloud-free reference images are difficult to obtain,
and SAR imagery is not well matched to the spatial resolution of
MODIS, making it difficult to provide effective prior constraints.
To address these issues, this study proposes a land-cover-guided
two-stage time series reconstruction network (LC-TSRNet) for
seamless reconstruction of annual MODIS SR products. In the first
stage, an annual land cover map is introduced as prior knowledge,
and a class-specific spectral prior module (CSPM) is designed
by leveraging the spectral similarity within identical land cover
types, thereby providing prior spectral constraints for missing
regions. In the second stage, to address large-area, persistent,
and overlapping gaps in MODIS imagery, a spatiotemporal multi-
scale residual module (SMRM) is designed to adaptively exploit
and integrate multi-scale spatiotemporal contextual information.
Meanwhile, to address the limited collaborative utilization of
spatio-temporal-spectral information, a three-dimensional atten-
tion module (3DAM) is designed to impose cross-dimensional joint
constraints. Considering the lack of publicly available MODIS
sample datasets, this study constructs the AIRMOD-TSR dataset,
which contains annual MODIS SR time series and corresponding
annual land cover maps. Extensive experimental results show
that LC-TSRNet outperforms the comparison methods and

I. Introduction
O
PTICAL satellite remote sensing has emerged as a fun-
damental instrument for Earth observation. The periodic
revisit of satellites enables the acquisition of time series imagery
over the same region, supporting continuous monitoring and
quantitative analysis of the Earth’s surface [1]. Time series
optical remote sensing data have been widely applied in
vegetation monitoring [2], [3], phenology extraction [4], [5],
land-use change analysis [6], [7] and disaster response [8],
[9]. Among various optical sensors, MODIS has become an
important data source for long-term, large-scale time series
studies because of its high temporal resolution, wide swath, and
long-term continuous observations capabilities [10]. Among
MODIS land products, the Surface Reflectance (SR) product
serves as the most fundamental standardized data, providing
critical inputs for higher-level products such as the Leaf Area
Index (LAI), Fraction of Absorbed Photosynthetically Active
Radiation (FPAR), and Net Primary Productivity (NPP).
However, MODIS SR imagery commonly suffers from
varying degrees of data gaps due to cloud contamination and
sensor malfunctions [11]. Long-term continuous observations
indicate that the average global cloud coverage for MODIS data
reaches approximately 67% [12]. This not only compromises
the overall quality but also disrupts the temporal continuity of
observations, thereby limiting the accuracy and reliability of
applications. In addition, SR exhibits pronounced intra-annual
dynamics driven by vegetation phenology and seasonal changes
[13]. Therefore, a time series reconstruction algorithm tailored
to MODIS SR products is required to generate seamless annual
SR time series while preserving intra-annual land surface
dynamics.
To improve the data quality of optical remote sensing
imagery, extensive research over the past decades has led to a
variety of missing reconstruction methods. Early approaches
can be categorized into spatial-based, temporal-based, and
spatiotemporal hybrid methods [11]. However, most of these

exhibit deficiencies in complex and fine-textured scenes, and

In recent years, deep learning technology has been regarded
as a new paradigm for missing reconstruction in remote sensing
imagery. According to input configurations, deep-learning-
based approaches can be categorized into single-image-based
and sequence-based methods. Single-image-based methods
typically reconstruct missing images by incorporating a nearby
cloud-free optical image or a synthetic aperture radar (SAR)
image as prior information [15], [16], [17], [18]. However,
extending these methods to time series is challenging, as each
missing image requires corresponding auxiliary data, and the
correlations between sequential images are largely ignored.
Sequence-based methods typically take multi-temporal image
sequences as input to fully exploit inter-image temporal
dynamics and spatial correlations, thereby substantially im-
proving reconstruction quality. Some studies use only cloud-
contaminated optical sequences with missing observations,
exploiting the cloud-free images to reconstruct the missing
content [19], [20], [14]. Recently, TAMGAN [21] uses a
GAN framework with a temporal attention mechanism to
reconstruct annual MODIS SR time series. Nevertheless, its
global spectral noise filling strategy may struggle to handle
completely missing observations, and its applicability to full
7-band MODIS SR products remains to be verified because
only four bands are reconstructed. In addition, some studies
address missing observations in optical time series imagery by
integrating SAR data [22], [23], [24].
Although sequence-based deep learning methods have made
substantial progress, MODIS SR time series reconstruction
still faces the following challenges. First, most sequence-based
methods rely on cloud-free observations from optical time
series or introduce SAR time series as auxiliary data, and they
have been developed and validated primarily on Sentinel and
Landsat imagery [14], [25]. However, due to the coarse spatial
resolution of MODIS, such as 500 m for the MOD09A1 SR
product, it is difficult to obtain cloud-free reference images
for reconstruction at the regional scale [26]. In this study, the
global land surface was divided into 6854 regions of 512 ×
512 pixels (approximately 65536 km2), and the annual mean
missing ratio of each region was derived from MOD09A1
data for 2019–2023. As shown in Fig. 1(a), the resulting
cumulative frequency distribution indicates that entirely cloud-
free time series are absent at the regional scale, while regions
with annual mean missing ratios below 10% account for only
4.66% of all global land regions. In addition, SAR imagery is
generally acquired at spatial resolutions ranging from meters
to tens of meters [27], which are not naturally compatible with
MODIS SR products. When SAR imagery is aggregated to the
spatial resolution of MODIS, its fine-scale textural features and
geometric structural details are substantially weakened [28],
making it difficult to provide effective auxiliary information.
Therefore, it is necessary to explore new prior constraints for
MODIS SR time series reconstruction.
Second, current reconstruction methods typically employ
fixed local receptive fields or static multi-scale structures for
feature extraction, but lack an effective mechanism to adaptively
exploit multi-scale spatiotemporal contextual information in
response to varying extents of missing data [29], [30]. MODIS
SR time series imagery often suffers from large-area gaps across
multiple consecutive periods, under which valid information
within local neighborhoods becomes severely insufficient
[31]. As a result, these methods struggle to establish long-
range spatiotemporal dependencies across missing regions,
making reconstruction particularly challenging under large-area,
persistent, and overlapping scenarios.
Third, existing methods still lack effective integration of
spatial, temporal, and spectral information, thereby neglecting
the intrinsic physical correlations among them [32], [33].
MODIS SR time series imagery contains highly coupled
spatial, temporal, and spectral information. Due to the lack of
joint cross-dimensional constraint, these methods struggle to
simultaneously preserve spatiotemporal continuity and spectral
fidelity during reconstruction.
Moreover, deep-learning-based reconstruction methods rely
on extensive training samples. Existing publicly available
satellite time series datasets for missing reconstruction are
mainly developed for Sentinel and Landsat imagery, such as
EarthNet2021 [34], SEN12MS-CR-TS [35], PASTIS-R [36],
and AllClear [37]. In contrast, MODIS SR time series sample
datasets remain scarce.


3
To address the above issues, this study proposes a novel
Land-Cover-guided two-stage Time Series Reconstruction
Network (LC-TSRNet), which simultaneously reconstructs
missing values across annual MOD09A1 8-day composite
SR time series. The proposed LC-TSRNet adopts a coarse-
to-fine two-stage architecture. In the first stage, the globally
available MCD12Q1 IGBP annual land cover map is introduced
as prior knowledge, and a class-specific spectral prior module
(CSPM) is designed to impose spectral constraints, thereby
generating a coarse representation. In the second stage, a
spatiotemporal multi-scale residual module (SMRM) and a
three-dimensional attention module (3DAM) are designed to
refine spatio-temporal-spectral features. Finally, seamless SR
time series with rich texture details are generated.
The main contributions of this study are as follows:
1) To impose prior constraints on MODIS SR time series
reconstruction, we incorporate land cover information and
design the CSPM. By leveraging the spectral similarity within
identical land cover types at each time step, the CSPM
transforms the semantic class knowledge into spectral prior
knowledge, thereby imposing spectral-radiometric constraints
on missing regions and improving the spectral fidelity of the
reconstructed SR time series.
2) The SMRM is designed to adaptively exploit multi-scale
spatiotemporal contextual information under varying extents of
missing data. By employing parallel 3D convolutional branches
with different dilation rates and a progressive aggregation
mechanism, the SMRM enables cross-scale spatiotemporal
interaction across missing regions, thereby providing effective
joint spatiotemporal constraints for reconstructing large-area,
persistent, and overlapping gaps.
3) The 3DAM is designed to adaptively enhance key features
across the temporal, spatial, and spectral dimensions. By
integrating three attention submodules in a parallel-cascaded
structure, the 3DAM establishes joint cross-dimensional con-
straints and strengthens the intrinsic correlations among spatio-
temporal-spectral features, thereby improving the spatiotempo-
ral continuity and spectral fidelity of reconstructed SR time
series.
4) To address the scarcity of publicly available MODIS SR
time series datasets, the AIRMOD-TSR dataset is constructed.
The dataset comprises annual MODIS SR time series and
corresponding annual land cover maps for 13 representative
regions of interest (ROIs) worldwide, providing a benchmark
for MODIS SR time series reconstruction.

II. Related Works
A. Early Approaches
Early research in missing reconstruction can generally be
classified into three categories: spatial-based, temporal-based,
and spatiotemporal hybrid methods.
1) Spatial-Based Methods: Spatial-based methods depend
entirely upon the valid pixels within incomplete image, recon-
structing gaps by exploiting image statistical characteristics
and spatial correlations. Spatial interpolation assumes local
continuity, inferring missing pixels by assigning weights to
neighboring pixels [38]. Typical methods include Kriging
interpolation [39], nearest-neighbor interpolation [40], and
spline interpolation [41]. Additionally, variation-based [42],
[43], exemplar-based [44], [45], and partial differential equation
(PDE)-based methods [46] have also been applied to missing
reconstruction. However, these methods are typically suitable
only for small-area gaps or homogeneous regions. They
exhibit significant limitations in accuracy and reliability when
reconstructing large-area gaps or complex-texture regions.
2) Temporal-Based Methods: Temporal-based methods typi-
cally require auxiliary images from other dates and reconstruct
gaps by exploiting temporal correlations across multi-temporal
observations. Representative methods include temporal filtering
[47], histogram matching [48], and regression-based methods
[49], [50]. For instance, Zeng et al. [49] proposed WLR, which
combines sequential reference observations with a reference-
free regularization framework. However, these methods typ-
ically require reference images that are temporally adjacent,
cloud-free, and highly consistent in land-cover content.
3) Spatiotemporal Hybrid Methods: Spatiotemporal hybrid
methods reconstruct missing area by combining spatial and
temporal correlations across multi-temporal images. To address
the missing data issue in Landsat ETM+ SLC-off imagery, Chen
et al. [51] developed NSPI. Building on this foundation, Zhu et
al. [52] further proposed a modified NSPI (MNSPI) by refining
similar-pixel selection and applying a weighted linear model
for thick cloud removal. Integrating local temporal correlations
with non-local spatial correlations, Li et al. [53] proposed
PM-MTGSR, a novel sparse representation approach driven
by patch-matching. Ng et al. [54] proposed AWTC, which
formulates gap filling as a weighted low-rank tensor recovery
task. Chen et al. [55] developed spatiotemporal weighted
regression (STWR) to estimate missing pixels. However,
such methods often struggle with fine-detail reconstruction
in complex, textured scenes and typically involve a high
computational cost.
B. Deep-Learning-Based Approaches
Deep-learning-based approaches can be broadly categorized
into single-image-based and sequence-based methods according
to their input configurations.
1) Single-Image-Based Methods: Single-image-based meth-
ods typically reconstruct missing observations by leveraging
a nearby cloud-free optical image or a SAR image as prior
information. For instance, Ma et al. [15] proposed TIIN, which
leverages spatial priors from adjacent optical images to improve
dehazing performance.

In addition, SAR can penetrate clouds and haze, enabling
surface observations under all-weather conditions, and is
therefore widely used to guide optical image reconstruction.
For instance, Wang et al. [16] proposed USSRN-CR for
thick cloud removal assisted by SAR data. Li et al. [17]
developed a two-stage optical-SAR fusion model (HF-T2CR)
that simultaneously removes thin and thick clouds. Pan et al.
[18] introduced Mamba for thick cloud removal and proposed
M3-CR. Single image-based methods have achieved notable
progress in missing reconstruction. However, these methods are
not well suited for time series reconstruction, as they neglect
sequential correlations and require corresponding auxiliary data
for each missing image.
2) Sequence-Based Methods: Sequence-based methods typ-
ically use multi-temporal image sequences as input to better
exploit temporal dynamics and spatial correlations across
observations. Some studies rely solely on optical image
sequences with gaps to reconstruct the missing content. For
example, Zhang et al. [19] developed PSTCR, which constructs
Sentinel-2 MSI and Landsat-8 OLI patch groups to extract
spatiotemporal features and effectively remove thick cloud.
Stucker et al. [20] proposed U-TILISE to reconstruct optical
time series imagery. It extracts spatial features with 2D
convolutions and temporal features with 1D self-attention,
thereby reconstructing gaps in Sentinel-2 time series imagery.
Zhang et al. [14] proposed MS2TAN, which leverages multi-
temporal information to convert incomplete sequences into
complete ones, and effectively validated it on Landsat and
Sentinel-2 data. Wang et al. [21] developed TAMGAN, a GAN-
based framework that uses a 3D-convolutional generator for
annual MODIS SR reconstruction.
Another category of methods addresses missing observations
in optical time series imagery by incorporating SAR data. For
instance, Zhang et al. [22] proposed U-SeqNet that combines
U-Net with a sequence-to-sequence architecture and exploits
SAR-optical multimodal information to restore Landsat-8 time
series images. Liu et al. [23] proposed STORI, which leverages
a bidirectional recurrent architecture to integrate Sentinel-1
and Sentinel-2 time series for robust optical reconstruction in
cloud-prone regions. Shu et al. [24] developed RESTORE-DiT,
a conditional diffusion framework that jointly uses Sentinel-1
SAR and Sentinel-2 optical time series to reconstruct high-
quality optical sequences. Tang et al. [25] developed Anytime-
Former, which fuses irregular Sentinel-2 optical observations
with asynchronous Sentinel-1 SAR time series to reconstruct
SR at arbitrary timestamps. However, methods based on optical
sequences or optical-SAR sequences typically rely heavily on
the quality of optical or SAR imagery.
III. Materials and Dataset Construction
In this study, the MOD09A1 8-day composite SR product
was utilized, as it effectively captures seasonal dynamics while
mitigating the severe data gaps inherent in daily observations
[56]. For the MCD12Q1 annual land cover product, the IGBP
classification scheme was adopted because of its widespread
use in global studies [57]. Given the scarcity of publicly
available MODIS SR time series datasets, AIRMOD-TSR was
constructed using Google Earth Engine (GEE).
A. Materials
The MOD09A1 SR product is provided at 500 m spatial
resolution, contains 46 composites per year, and includes seven
bands spanning the visible to shortwave infrared (SWIR): B1
(620–670 nm), B2 (841–876 nm), B3 (459–479 nm), B4 (545–
565 nm), B5 (1230–1250 nm), B6 (1628–1652 nm), and B7
(2105–2155 nm). Correspondingly, the MCD12Q1 product
provides annual land cover maps at the same 500 m spatial
resolution, which is spatially consistent with the MOD09A1
SR product and readily available, making it highly suitable as
prior information for MODIS SR time series reconstruction.

construct AIRMOD-TSR for training and evaluation.

IV. Methodology
A. Overview
The proposed LC-TSRNet adopts a coarse-to-fine two-stage
architecture for seamless reconstruction of annual MODIS SR
time series. As illustrated in Fig. 4, the network consists of
two stages: a land-cover-guided coarse representation stage and
a spatio-temporal-spectral joint refinement stage. Considering
the temporal characteristics of MODIS data, the SR time series
with missing observations is organized as an input tensor 𝑋𝑠𝑟∈
R𝐶×𝑇×𝐻×𝑊, where 𝐶and 𝑇denote the number of channels
and time steps, respectively, and 𝐻and 𝑊denote the image
height and width. The corresponding annual land cover map
is represented as 𝑋𝑙𝑐∈R1×1×𝐻×𝑊and input to the model.
Furthermore, considering that temporal variations in SR exhibit
inherent regularity and strong correlations [6], all convolutional
layers in LC-TSRNet employ 3D convolutions, as illustrated
in Fig. 5(a).
In the land-cover-guided coarse representation stage, a dual-
branch architecture is designed to process the temporally
incomplete SR time series and the prior reference SR, thereby
generating a coarse representation. Specifically, the CSPM trans-
forms the static land cover map into a full-year reference SR
time series, providing physically consistent spectral constraints
for missing regions in accordance with land cover dynamics,
while standard convolutional (Conv) blocks are employed for
feature extraction, as illustrated in Fig. 5(b). Meanwhile, the
temporally incomplete SR branch adopts gated convolution
(GConv) blocks to generate adaptive gating weights and extract
valid features, as illustrated in Fig. 5(c). Finally, the output
features from the two branches are progressively integrated by
the progressive fusion module (PFM) to form an initial coarse
representation.
In the spatio-temporal-spectral joint refinement stage, multi-
ple modules are jointly employed to impose spatio-temporal-
spectral constraints on the coarse representation obtained from
the first stage, thereby generating seamless SR time series
with improved spatiotemporal continuity. First, the SMRM
adaptively exploits multi-scale information according to the
extent of missing data and enables deep cross-scale interaction
of spatiotemporal contextual information through a progressive
aggregation mechanism. Next, the 3DAM adaptively focuses
on salient features across the temporal, spatial, and spectral
dimensions and imposes joint cross-dimensional constraints,
thereby enhancing local textures and structural details. Then,
ConvLSTM is employed to model long-range temporal depen-
dencies and ensure temporal consistency in the reconstructed
sequences. Finally, multiple cascaded Conv blocks are used
to map high-dimensional features back to the original data
space, while residual skip connections are introduced to
preserve information in valid regions. Through the coordinated
refinement of these modules, the network outputs seamless SR
time series ˆ𝑋∈R𝐶×𝑇×𝐻×𝑊with 46 composites and 7 bands.

1) CSPM: To impose effective prior constraints in MODIS
SR time series reconstruction, land cover information is
introduced as prior knowledge. The MCD12Q1 product, as an
annual land cover dataset, is well matched to the MOD09A1
SR product in spatial resolution and is readily available.
Considering the significant spectral differences among different
land cover classes and the relative spectral consistency within
the same class [58], the CSPM is designed. The CSPM
transforms semantic class knowledge from land cover maps
into reference SR time series knowledge, thereby imposing
spectral-radiometric constraints on missing regions that are
consistent with the temporal dynamics of specific land cover
types.

2) GConv Block: Optical imagery often contains a large
proportion of invalid pixels. When standard convolutions are
directly applied, features from invalid regions may propagate
through the network, causing noise accumulation and con-
taminating valid representations, which ultimately degrades
reconstruction quality. To alleviate this issue, GConv blocks are
introduced in the incomplete SR time series branch. Compared
with the Conv block, the GConv block uses a sigmoid
activation to generate explicit gating weights, thereby adaptively
suppressing invalid regions and highlighting reliable pixels.
This design enhances local spatiotemporal feature extraction
and improves robustness under irregular gaps and persistently
overlapping missing regions, as illustrated in Fig. 5(c).
3) PFM: To obtain a unified coarse representation, the PFM
is designed to integrate features from the dual branches, as
illustrated in Fig. 7. Based on a progressive fusion strategy,
the PFM performs element-wise addition on multi-level feature
outputs to achieve complementary information integration,
effectively compensating for missing regions while preserving
reliable observations. Furthermore, the PFM preserves the dual-
branch design, using Conv blocks and GConv blocks to extract
features from the reference SR and incomplete SR branches,
respectively.

C. Stage Two: Spatio-Temporal-Spectral Joint Refinement
In the spatio-temporal-spectral joint refinement stage, the
coarse representation generated in the first stage is taken as
input, aiming to achieve refined reconstruction through joint
spatio-temporal-spectral constraints and to generate seamless
SR time series with 46 composites and 7 bands. This stage
mainly consists of three core components: SMRM, 3DAM, and
ConvLSTM.
Specifically, the SMRM is used to adaptively exploit multi-
scale spatiotemporal contextual information, the 3DAM is
employed to adaptively enhance salient features across the
temporal, spatial, and spectral dimensions, and the ConvLSTM
is used to model long-range temporal dependencies. Through
the coordinated refinement of these modules, this stage further
improves the spatiotemporal continuity, spectral fidelity, and
texture detail representation of the reconstructed results.
1) SMRM: SR time series imagery exhibits pronounced
multi-scale characteristics, where different land cover types
show variability in spatial scales and temporal dynamics, while
the extent of missing data is also unevenly distributed across
spatiotemporal dimensions. Existing studies have demonstrated
that multi-scale features play a crucial role in improving
reconstruction quality [59], [60]. However, most existing
methods rely on fixed local receptive fields or static multi-
scale structures for feature extraction, lacking mechanisms to
adaptively exploit multi-scale information according to the
extent of missing data. As a result, these methods struggle
to establish long-range spatiotemporal dependencies across
large-area, persistent, and overlapping gaps.
To address the above issues, the SMRM is proposed in
this study. To adaptively exploit multi-scale spatiotemporal
contextual information based on the coarse representation
obtained in the first stage, the SMRM introduces a progressive
aggregation mechanism that progressively propagates and fuses
the outputs of coarse-scale branches into finer-scale branches.
This design enables the model to adaptively leverage contextual
information at different scales according to the extent of
missing data: it relies more on large-scale context in regions

3) ConvLSTM: ConvLSTM [64] is introduced in the refine-
ment stage to further model long-range temporal dependencies.
Unlike standard LSTM [65], which models temporal depen-
dencies through fully connected transformations, ConvLSTM
replaces these operations with convolutional transformations,
enabling temporal dependency modeling while preserving
spatial structure. Therefore, ConvLSTM is well suited for
MODIS SR time series reconstruction. By incorporating
ConvLSTM, the model further enhances temporal consistency
in the annual seamless SR time series.

V. Experiments and Results
A. Experimental Settings
1) Dataset Settings: All experiments are conducted on
AIRMOD-TSR. R1–R8 are used for model training and
validation, containing 4950 samples in total. These samples
are divided into training and validation sets at a ratio of 4 : 1.
To enhance sample diversity, data augmentation is performed
during training by randomly rotating each input by 0◦, 90◦,
180◦, or 270◦.
Three high-quality ROIs are used as independent test regions
under different missing scenarios. R9 represents the spatially
adjacent scenario, as it is geographically close to the training
ROIs. R10 represents the cross-hemisphere scenario, where
seasonal phase inversion increases the difficulty of phenologi-
cal reconstruction. R11 represents the persistent overlapping
missing scenario for evaluating model robustness.
For all test scenarios, real MODIS missing masks obtained
from GEE are applied to the high-quality ROIs to simulate
realistic missing patterns. Fig. 10 shows the regional temporal
missing ratios and pixel-level annual missing ratios after mask
simulation. The temporal missing ratios range from 1.14%
to 100% for R9, from 1.13% to 100% for R10, and from
21.69% to 79.11% for R11. In R11, consecutive missing masks
are further used to simulate persistent overlapping gaps. To
facilitate visual comparison, three representative dates with
different missing ratios and phenological stages are selected
for each scenario, as indicated by the stars in Fig. 10.
2) Implementation Details: The model is implemented using
PyTorch 2.5.1. Training and testing are performed on an Ubuntu
22.04 workstation equipped with NVIDIA RTX 4090 GPUs.
The model is trained for 80 epochs with a batch size of 6
and an initial learning rate of 0.001. A validation-loss-based
adaptive decay schedule is adopted, where the learning rate
is reduced by a factor of 0.5 if the validation loss does not
improve for four consecutive epochs. The Adam optimizer is
used with 𝛽1 = 0.9 and 𝛽2 = 0.99. The loss balancing factor
𝜆is set to 0.2.
3) Comparison Methods: Six representative and widely used
reconstruction methods are selected for comparison, including
two conventional methods, WLR [49] and MNSPI [52], and four
deep learning methods, PSTCR [19], U-TILISE [20], TAMGAN
[21], and MS2TAN [14]. WLR is treated as a single-image-
based method, whereas the remaining methods are sequence-
based. For MODIS SR time series reconstruction, WLR uses
the composite with the lowest missing ratio among the 46
composites as the reference image, while PSTCR divides the
sequence into five-composite subsets according to acquisition
time and performs group-wise reconstruction.
4) Evaluation Metrics: To quantitatively evaluate reconstruc-
tion performance, four widely used metrics are adopted: peak
signal-to-noise ratio (PSNR) [67], structural similarity index
measure (SSIM) [66], correlation coefficient (CC) [68], and
spectral angle mapper (SAM) [69]. Specifically, PSNR and CC

E. Ablation studies
To investigate the contributions of individual modules and
the land cover prior, ablation studies are conducted under
the spatially adjacent scenario (R9) and the cross-hemisphere
scenario (R10). When the land cover prior is removed, only
incomplete SR time series are used as input. When CSPM
is removed, land cover class labels are directly used as input.
When SMRM is removed, only a single branch with a dilation
rate of 1 is retained. When 3DAM is removed, the multi-scale
representations are first refined by a convolution unit and then
fed into ConvLSTM to model long-term temporal dependencies.
1) Quantitative Results: Table V summarizes the ablation
results of five settings: removing the land cover prior, CSPM,
SMRM, and 3DAM, and using the complete LC-TSRNet.
The results show that removing any component degrades the
reconstruction performance on both R9 and R10, confirming
the effectiveness of each component in the proposed frame-
work. Among them, the land cover prior and CSPM provide
particularly notable improvements. For the geographically
distant R10 region, introducing the land cover prior improves
PSNR by 6.60% and reduces SAM by 13.19% compared with
using only incomplete SR time series. Furthermore, compared
with directly using land cover labels, CSPM further improves
PSNR by 12.26% and reduces SAM by 27.53%. These results
indicate that land cover information enhances cross-region
reconstruction, while CSPM further improves spectral fidelity
by converting semantic labels into spectral priors.

VI. Discussion
A. Reconstruction Performance under Real-world Scenario
To evaluate reconstruction performance under real-world
observation conditions, R12 is selected as the ROI for real-
data experiments. In 2022, the average missing ratio of the
46 composites in R12 reaches 62.81%, with several dates
approaching complete missingness.
Fig. 20 shows the masked real observed SR time series
on R12 and the corresponding reconstruction results of LC-
TSRNet. The real observations exhibit a distinct temporally
uneven missing patterns, with relatively complete observations
on some dates and severe data gaps on others. In particular,
large-area gaps persist for nearly two consecutive months
from 20220101 to 20220226, posing a substantial challenge to
the reconstruction capability of the model. Nevertheless, LC-
TSRNet is still able to reconstruct spatially continuous images,
with smooth transitions between reconstructed regions and
originally valid observations and without obvious boundary
artifacts. In addition, the reconstructed images on adjacent
dates maintain good consistency in texture structures and color
information. For other severely missing dates, such as 20220509,
20220525, 20221016, and 20221227, LC-TSRNet also achieves
satisfactory reconstruction results. These results indicate that
LC-TSRNet has strong robustness and practical applicability
under real-world observation conditions, providing an effective
solution for generating seamless MOD09A1 SR product from
incomplete observations.

imagery for vegetation monitoring and the temporal spectral
fidelity of reconstructed SR data. To this end, the R13 region

C. Limitations and Future Work
Although LC-TSRNet performs well in MODIS SR time se-
ries reconstruction, it still has several limitations. First, the land
cover product and the SR product are mismatched in temporal
scale. MCD12Q1 is provided annually, whereas MOD09A1 is
an 8-day composite product with 46 observations per year.
The ablation results in Section V-E confirm that a single
annual land cover map can provide effective prior information;
however, abrupt intra-annual land surface changes, such as
seasonal snow/ice cover and wildfire disturbances, cannot be
fully captured. This temporal mismatch may introduce biases
into the reference SR and subsequently affect reconstruction
accuracy.
Second, although the proposed method shows robustness un-
der large-area, persistent, and overlapping gaps, long periods of
complete data gaps remain challenging, such as those frequently
observed in the Amazon Basin. When valid observations are
absent for extended periods, information from neighboring
dates and spatial contexts becomes insufficient. Under such
conditions, reconstruction relies more heavily on the reference
SR derived from the land cover map, which may lead to
deviations from the actual surface conditions.
Future work will focus on addressing these limitations.
Additional prior information, such as meteorological variables
and topographic features, will be incorporated to further
improve reconstruction fidelity. In addition, the dataset will
be expanded to include a broader range of challenging global
scenarios, such as prolonged complete cloud cover and seasonal
snow/ice contamination, with the aim of further enhancing the
generalization capability and robustness of the proposed method
in real-world applications.
VII. Conclusion
In this study, a land-cover-guided two-stage method (LC-
TSRNet) is proposed for seamless reconstruction of annual
MODIS SR products. In the first stage, an annual land cover
map is introduced, and the CSPM is designed to transform
land cover classes into reference SR time series, thereby
providing prior spectral constraints for missing regions and
generating coarse representations. This alleviates the lack of
reliable prior constraints in MODIS SR reconstruction. In the
second stage, the SMRM adaptively exploits and integrates
multi-scale spatiotemporal contextual information through
parallel multi-scale branches and a progressive aggregation
mechanism, enabling the effective reconstruction of large-area,
persistent, and overlapping gaps. Subsequently, the 3DAM
imposes cross-dimensional joint constraints through three
parallel-cascaded attention submodules, thereby enabling the
collaborative utilization of spatio-temporal-spectral information
in SR time series. As a result, seamless SR time series with
rich texture details and high spectral consistency are generated.
In addition, considering the lack of publicly available MODIS
sample datasets, the AIRMOD-TSR dataset is constructed. It
contains annual MODIS SR time series and corresponding
annual land cover maps for 13 representative ROIs worldwide.
Extensive experiments on AIRMOD-TSR demonstrate that LC-
TSRNet outperforms the comparison methods in reconstruction
accuracy and exhibits stronger generalization capability and
robustness. The vegetation monitoring application further
verifies the temporal spectral fidelity and practical value of the
proposed method.
